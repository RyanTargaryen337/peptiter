## 1. What the product is

- **Name:** PepTiter
- **Name rationale:** *Pep* (peptide) + *titer* (a measure of concentration).
  The name is meant to reinforce the product's core positioning: precision and
  accurate dosing math.
- **What it is:** A free, web-based peptide tracker.
- **Status:** Idea stage. This website is the first step — nothing is built yet.
- **The core promise:** Remove the guesswork for beginners and prevent dosing
  errors through accurate reconstitution math.
- **What it combines in one place:**
  1. A peptide library
  2. A reconstitution / dosing calculator
  3. A personal progress log

---

## 2. Who it serves

The audience is not one blended group. Frame the site around distinct **paths**,
so each type of visitor feels the product is built for them:

- **Medical weight-loss** — people using GLP-1s. Often health-motivated and
  doctor-adjacent; a different mindset from the performance crowd.
- **Performance** — the gym & bodybuilding community.
- **Recovery** — people using healing / recovery peptides.
- **Longevity** — biohackers and enthusiasts.

Underlying all four: **anyone self-administering peptides.**

---

## 3. What the site contains

- **Peptide library** with per-peptide guides. Each guide covers: what the
  peptide is, how to reconstitute it, common protocols, cycle duration, and
  on-cycle care.
- **Reconstitution + dosing calculator** — turns concentration math into syringe
  units. **Usable instantly, before any signup.** The utility is open; the
  account is only the reason to *save* your work.
- **Progress logging** — weight, photos, notes.
- **Educational blog / articles.**
- **Sourcing & purity thread** — guidance on third-party lab testing and safe
  sourcing. Fake and contaminated product is one of the biggest real risks in
  this space; this content is a core trust and safety feature, not an add-on.

## 4. What a visitor should do

- **Primary call to action:** Create a free account.
- **Important nuance:** The account **gates saving your log, not the calculator
  itself.** A visitor can run the reconstitution math immediately, with no wall.
  Signing up is what lets them save protocols and track progress over time.
- **Supporting value proposition:** Everything in one place · Genuinely free ·
  Beginner-friendly.

## 5. Positioning, tone & safety

These are **core facts, treated as visible parts of the product — not fine
print.**

- **Educational only.** The site is explicitly **not medical advice.**
- **Harm-reduction framing.** The product meets people where they are, assumes
  some will proceed regardless, and helps them do it more safely. Accurate dosing
  math is the central harm-reduction feature, because dosing errors are the
  single biggest risk in self-administration.
- **A plain-language "How we think about this" statement, up front.** Short,
  honest, visible: harm-reduction, sources cited, not medical advice. This is
  what separates PepTiter from anonymous forum threads and builds trust.
- **Framing of dosing content:** The site **"shows common protocols and
  calculates yours"** — it does **not** "recommend" or "prescribe" a dose. It is
  a reference plus a calculator, not a prescriber.
- **Tone:** Calm, precise, clean. *Modern lab tool meets clean fitness app.*
  Whitespace and restraint. No neon "GAINS" energy, no fear-mongering. Measured
  reads as trustworthy — which is everything for a health tool.

## 7. Factual background on the subject

Real, verified background to inform the build (not invented):

- **Peptide categories in this space:**
  - **GLP-1 receptor agonists** — e.g. semaglutide, tirzepatide.
  - **Growth-hormone secretagogues** — e.g. ipamorelin, CJC-1295, sermorelin.
  - **Recovery peptides** — e.g. BPC-157, TB-500.
  - **Others** — e.g. PT-141, melanotan.
- **A real legal / safety distinction the site must reflect:** GLP-1s are
  prescription medications. Many other peptides are sold as "research chemicals"
  that are **not approved for human consumption.** These are not the same thing,
  and the site should not blur them.
- **Reconstitution basics:** Lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides are
  reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and typically dosed via an insulin
  syringe measured in units.
- **Harm-reduction concerns beyond dosing:** sterile technique, refrigerated
  storage after reconstitution, sourcing, and third-party purity testing.

## 8. Content boundary for the build (read this)

Specific **dosage numbers, cycle lengths, and mg-per-protocol values** for each
peptide are content the site *will hold* — but they are **not** included in this
document, and must **not** be fabricated.

- "Per-peptide dosing guide" is a **required content type**, listed here as a
  placeholder to be filled.
- The actual figures must come from **verified sources supplied by the product
  owner**, not invented during the build.
- Anywhere the calculator or a guide would display a protocol figure, treat it as
  a slot fed by sourced data — never a hard-coded guess.
